Practice Areas

A Plan for Your Family,
Not Just a Collection of Documents

Clear, practical estate planning designed to protect your family, preserve control, and make difficult moments easier to navigate.

01

Wills

Nominate guardians for your children, choose who will handle your estate, and make your wishes clear for the people you leave behind.

A will provides instructions for what should happen after your death, including who should administer your estate and who you would want to care for your minor children. For parents, nominating trusted guardians is one of the most important decisions an estate plan can address.

02

Revocable Living Trusts

Maintain control of your assets during your lifetime, plan for incapacity, and help your family avoid probate when the time comes.

A revocable living trust allows you to manage your assets during your lifetime, provides a plan for their management if you become incapacitated, and directs how they should be distributed after your death. When properly created and funded, a trust can help your family avoid probate while providing for your spouse, children, and other beneficiaries according to your wishes.

03

Powers of Attorney

Make sure someone you trust can step in to manage important financial and legal matters if you're unable to do so yourself.

Without proper authority in place, even a spouse or close family member may face limitations when trying to manage certain financial or legal matters on your behalf. A durable power of attorney helps ensure that the person you choose has the authority needed to act when necessary, reducing the risk of delay, uncertainty, or court involvement during an already difficult time.

04

Advance Health Care Directives

Choose someone you trust to make health care decisions if you're unable to do so yourself, and give your family clear guidance about the care you want.

Medical decisions can become especially difficult when family members are unsure what you would have wanted. An advance health care directive lets you document your preferences for medical care and gives a trusted person the authority to speak for you when necessary, providing clarity during an already difficult time.
